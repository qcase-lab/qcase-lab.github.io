Research Focus & Impact

Where Software Engineering, AI, also known as Super Intelligence (SI), and Quantum Computing meet

Housed in the School of Applied and Creative Computing (ACC) in the Purdue Polytechnic Institute (PPI), Purdue University, on both of the Purdue West Lafayette (PWL) and Purdue Indianapolis (PIN) campuses, in Indiana, USA, the Purdue QCASE Lab focuses on research and innovation at the intersection of Software Engineering (SE), including Software Security; Artificial Intelligence (AI), with a concentration on the state of the art in machine learning, including Large Language Models and agentic AI; and Quantum Computing (QC), primarily Quantum Software Engineering (QSE) and Quantum AI (QAI).

The overarching themes of our research are AI for Software Engineering (AI4SE), including but not limited to AI for Software Security and Software Supply Chain Security, and Software Engineering for AI (SE4AI), an emerging field known as AI Engineering, for classical, quantum, and hybrid quantum-classical software systems. Supporting the software development process and enhancing the quality of software systems, including software-intensive systems with modern and emerging technologies and platforms, are very interesting to the Purdue QCASE Lab.

The research program in the Purdue QCASE Lab is at the heart of an organic, symbiotic ecosystem comprising the lab, classroom, and community. We value training, mentoring, and community impact. Formerly known as the QAS Lab, founded in August 2023 at the University of Colorado, the research program transferred to Purdue University in August 2026 with the move of its Director, Dr. Armin Moin, from the University of Colorado to Purdue University.

Projects

AI for Quantum Circuit Security, Optimization, Compilation, and Transpilation

We develop and adapt static and dynamic analysis methods (some based on AI) to enhance the security of quantum programs following the gate-based/circuit-based model of computation. We also develop and apply various AI methods, such as Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Generative Pretrained Transformer Large Language Models (LLMs), to make quantum circuit optimization, compilation, and transpilation more effective and efficient. See also the Q-Dev project.

AI for Software (Supply Chain) Security

We study how advanced machine learning methods, such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and Generative Pretrained Transformer Large Language Models (LLMs) can be used to detect and mitigate software vulnerabilities in software supply chains.

Peer-reviewed publications: FSE 2026 . ICSE 2026

AI for Software Maintenance

We develop and apply various AI methods, such as Generative Pretrained Transformer Large Language Models (LLMs), for Mining Software Repositories (MSR), for example, to make bug triage in large open-source software projects more efficient. See also TianoShield.

Peer-reviewed publications: COMPSAC 2025

Applied AI Collaborations

Broader use cases of applied AI with other research groups, such as in Bioinformatics and smart grids. For example, we develop and apply new AI methods to help our collaborators study dendritic branching in neurons for disease manifestation detection and prediction.
